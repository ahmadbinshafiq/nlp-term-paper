The log-primary family is now well populated with systems (ActiveGraph, ESAA, OpenHands SDK, agrepl, Temporal, Agent Flight Recorder), but every one of them reports engineering metrics (replay fidelity, per-event overhead, case-study event counts) rather than audit-task outcomes; nobody has measured failure-localization accuracy, ACR/RF/LPC/SPDR-style recoverability, or evidence-tracing coverage on a log-primary runtime against a checkpoint-based one on the same workflow, which is exactly the controlled comparison the exposé proposes. The counterfactual-analysis literature (Causal Agent Replay, REFLECT, DoVer, AgentCheck) presupposes a replayable record but is architecture-agnostic and never asks whether the substrate (event log vs checkpoints vs OTel-span overlay) changes attribution accuracy or cost, while two papers supply ready-made limits to test: replay cost grows linearly with candidate events (Zero-Replay Debugging) and static replay after a substituted step scores the wrong world with only 3% of replayed states valid (Replay Gap). Log growth and compaction are acknowledged everywhere (ActiveGraph replays a million-event run in full; Temporal hard-caps history at 51,200 events and resets via Continue-As-New; Beyond Compaction argues summarization destroys causal structure) but no one measures how snapshotting or compaction degrades audit properties, which suggests treating log-primary vs checkpoint-based as a continuum with snapshot interval as the independent variable and reconstruction fidelity, storage, and replay time as dependent variables. Evidence tracing at the claim level is essentially absent from this family (logs record model calls, not claim-to-source entailment), yet Agent Flight Recorder's result that structured events give forensic-query precision 1.0 versus 0.013 to 0.077 for text search hints at a testable hypothesis about structured-log queries versus a separately built provenance overlay. A single-semester hypothesis that fits this gap: on one fixed workflow (e.g., the ActiveGraph diligence-pack example or an AgentCheck-perturbed MCP task) with injected ground-truth faults, an LLM auditor given the event log localizes failures and reconstructs executions at least as accurately as one given LangGraph checkpoints or OTel spans, but counterfactual forks from the log lose validity with fork depth as the Replay Gap predicts, and compaction below some snapshot interval measurably lowers reconstruction fidelity while cutting storage.

The counterfactual/what-if literature now falls into three cost regimes that map almost one-to-one onto the exposé's three families, yet nobody has compared them on the same workflow: (1) observed counterfactuals with a recorded prefix and a re-executed suffix (CAR, CausalFlow, DoVer, agent-timetravel, LangGraph fork, AgentRewind), costing O(steps x K) model calls and zero calls for the prefix; (2) environment-level snapshot/rollback (DeltaBox, Crab, TClone) that makes the sandbox side of a fork ms-cheap but says nothing about auditing; and (3) simulated or predicted counterfactuals over the log only (CHIEF, Zero-Replay) that cost nothing at query time but are approximations whose fidelity is measured only against their own replay oracle. The Replay Gap paper (COLM 2026) adds a sharp empirical result that undermines naive log-primary forking: replaying a logged trajectory with a changed decision leaves only ~3% of states valid, and temperature-0 determinism itself depends on the serving stack (FP8 vs AWQ), so ActiveGraph's 'strict replay = proof of reproducibility' has never been stress-tested. A concrete, cheap term-paper hypothesis therefore is: 'On a fixed tool-using workflow with Who&When-style planted failures, a log-primary architecture with content-addressed caching reaches the same step-level attribution accuracy as checkpoint-based re-execution at a fraction of the model-call cost, but only while tools are side-effect-free; once tools are stateful, checkpoint+sandbox-snapshot forks dominate and log-only simulated counterfactuals (CHIEF-style) lose most of the step-level accuracy.' Nobody has reported the fidelity-vs-cost curve of these three regimes side by side, the interaction between cached prefixes and the statistical-independence requirement of resampling at step k (Mnimi) is unexamined, and none of the counterfactual works touch the other audit tasks (evidence tracing, policy compliance), which leaves room for a small controlled comparison measured with the Auditable-Agents (ACR/RF/LPC) and AAR (PCov/PSnd) metrics the student already has.

Verdict: after 20 searches with varied phrasing, I found no head-to-head empirical comparison of log-primary vs checkpoint-based vs provenance-overlay architectures on agent audit tasks. The closest works are (a) the June 2026 survey arXiv 2606.04990, which taxonomizes structured logs / execution graphs / evidence graphs / runtime provenance and states the storage-privacy-granularity trade-off only qualitatively while explicitly listing the absence of end-to-end and recovery-oriented benchmarks as an open challenge; (b) Vispute and Kadam (2603.21692), who argue that checkpoints and traces cannot reconstruct reasoning provenance but never measure it; (c) Rosen and Rosen (2605.06365), the only controlled architecture-vs-baseline experiment, which measures reproducibility on two memo tasks rather than audit tasks; and (d) Wu et al. 'Safe to Resume?' (2608.29381), which characterizes the checkpoint/rollback design space and its failure modes from a security angle. Existing quantitative evidence that record architecture matters is indirect and single-axis: TraceElephant shows full vs partial traces change failure-attribution accuracy by up to 76%, The Replay Gap shows static log replay mispredicts counterfactual outcomes versus live branching, and Repair-or-Resample shows unguided reruns reproduce failures only ~68% of the time. The systems-provenance literature (Stamatogiannakis et al. 2016, ProvMark 2019, Grayson et al. 2024) already has the exact experimental template (same workload, several capture architectures, measure granularity, expressiveness, overhead) but has never been applied to LLM agents; porting that design, with Who&When/TRAIL for localization ground truth, Causal Agent Replay for counterfactuals, and the Auditable Agents ACR/RF/SPDR metrics or ProvMark-style decidability scoring for reconstruction and policy checking, is a clear, tractable and novel term-paper contribution. A second, cheaper gap is that no work reports storage size, replay cost and privacy exposure for the same run encoded three ways; even a small controlled workflow instrumented with an event log, a LangGraph-style checkpointer and a PROV overlay would be the first such measurement.

The overlay literature splits cleanly into INLINE overlays (PROV-AGENT/Flowcept, VeriGraph, LEDGER, Agent-BOM, AuthGraph, DeepEvidence) and POST-HOC overlays reconstructed from traces (GRADE, FALAT, EDGE, Adaptive Influence Graphs, AgentTether, IBM's task-flow discovery), yet no paper measures how much of an inline-captured dependency graph a post-hoc reconstruction from OTel/Langfuse-style spans actually recovers; GRADE explicitly says ground-truth reliance labels do not exist at scale, which is a gap a single controlled workflow with both capture modes could fill. No paper compares an overlay against an event-log or checkpoint baseline on the same run and the same audit tasks: the only cross-family numbers are the Oracle AER paper's self-described stylized storage comparison (checkpoints ~560 KB vs 25-130 KB per 10-step run), and the survey confirms no benchmark covers provenance relations end to end. The two overlay sub-communities also use disjoint metrics (grounding rate / PCov for evidence tracing vs Who&When, TRAIL and MAST accuracy for failure localization), so nobody has scored one architecture on both audit tasks. Counterfactual analysis is nearly absent from overlay work (only EDGE's counterfactual rollout and CTA's paired-trace alignment), which is exactly where log-primary fork/replay (ActiveGraph) should have a structural advantage, an untested claim. A tractable term-paper hypothesis therefore is: on one LangGraph-style workflow instrumented three ways (append-only event log, per-step checkpoints, inline PROV overlay via Flowcept or a small PROV-DM emitter), a post-hoc GRADE-style overlay reconstructed from the span tree will match the inline overlay on execution reconstruction (ACR/RF, LPC) but fall short on failure localization and evidence tracing because the missing data-dependency edges are precisely the observed/declared ones, while costing far less storage than checkpoints; the span-tree-only baselines (OTel GenAI, OpenInference, Langfuse) make this cheap to set up.

Three metric frameworks now exist for agent auditability (Nian et al.'s five dimensions with ACR/RF/LPC/GB/SPDR/ADL/AC/ACD/IS/VC; Rasheed et al.'s AAR with PCov/PSnd/CTran/AEff; Solozobov's DEMM sufficiency verdicts), plus older qualitative ones (Fernsel's claims/evidence/accessibility, Chan's identifiers/monitoring/logging, Mökander's three layers, SMACTR), but none has been computed end-to-end on one real system: Nian et al. say so explicitly in their limitations, AAR reports no measurements at all, Fernsel is a qualitative case study, and DEMM-Bench uses 64 synthetic cases from a single author. Almost all measured work is failure attribution over a FIXED trace (Who&When 53.5%/14.2%, TRAIL ~11%, AgentRx +75%, CatchBench, FALAT), so the recording architecture is never a controlled variable; the only architecture-sensitive evidence is TraceElephant (full vs partial trace, up to +76% relative) and DEMM-Bench's eight evidence regimes, neither of which runs the same workflow under different architectures. Evidence integrity and cost are measured only as micro-benchmarks on synthetic workloads (Agent Flight Recorder 48 us/event, 512 B/event; AuditWeave tens of us/event; Aegis 8.3 ms), while the provenance-overlay representative PROV-AGENT lists five audit queries but reports no overhead, and the checkpoint family (OpenRath, LangGraph time travel, AER) has no published audit measurements whatsoever. Evidence tracing (PCov/PSnd), counterfactual analysis and policy checkability (SPDR) have no public benchmark tied to agent records; counterfactual replay appears only as a data-generation trick (Who&When Pro, AgenTracer) or a localization heuristic (FALAT). A feasible, novel term-paper hypothesis therefore is: run one controlled agent workflow under a log-primary (ActiveGraph or a hash-chained JSONL), a checkpoint-based (LangGraph checkpointer/OpenRath-style snapshots) and a provenance-overlay (Flowcept/PROV-AGENT or OTel GenAI spans) recorder, inject faults via replay-then-inject, and compute the same small metric set (ACR/RF/LPC/SPDR from Nian, PCov and AEff path-length from AAR, step-level localization accuracy from Who&When, PROV-AGENT's Q1-Q5 answerability, plus bytes/event and per-step latency); no paper found has done this, and Nian et al. name it as the missing experiment.

Evidence-tracing evaluation is mature at the output level (AIS, ALCE, AttributionBench, CiteEval, FActScore, ExpertQA, RAGBench all supply gold claim-evidence support labels and NLI/LLM judges) and is now being applied to whole deep-research reports (DeepTRACE, ReportBench, DeepResearch Bench FACT, DeepFact, Cited-but-Not-Verified), but nearly all of it is post-hoc over the final text and never asks what the agent's execution record (append-only log, step checkpoints, or a provenance overlay) contributes to tracing a claim to its source. The exceptions that do look inside the run (VeriTrail's intermediate-output datasets, TELBench/DRIFT and DeepHalluBench trajectory annotations) show that intermediate state is needed to find where unsupported content entered, yet none of them compares record architectures, and the only agent-specific overlay system found (LEDGER, Aug 2026) has no quantitative evaluation at all, mirroring ActiveGraph's own admission of no empirical study. The June 2026 survey by Wang et al. names precisely this hole: 'chronological logging vs semantic provenance' and 'granularity vs storage/privacy/annotation burden' are listed as unresolved trade-offs with no benchmark that labels typed provenance relations or measures whether provenance actually speeds recovery. A modest, single-semester contribution therefore fits: run one fixed research workflow (prompts from DeepResearch Bench or ReportBench, or replayed TELBench trajectories), capture it three ways (event log with replay, periodic state checkpoints, PROV/LEDGER-style claim-evidence overlay), and measure PCov, PSnd (calibrated on AttributionBench or DeepFact-Bench, keeping a small human-checked subset because LLM judges only reach ~0.62 correlation in DeepTRACE and ~80% F1 in AttributionBench), audit effort (path length / minutes), and storage overhead per claim; a testable hypothesis is that the overlay wins on evidence tracing and audit effort while the log wins on execution reconstruction and counterfactual forking, with checkpoints trading storage for neither. Two cheap add-ons that no reviewed paper combines with architecture choice are URL-existence checks at retrieval time (Rao et al.) and the 'evidence-force' stress test (FORCEBENCH), which would let the paper report evidence integrity and judge robustness alongside the architecture comparison.

The checkpoint family is now well represented, but in two disconnected literatures: official framework docs (LangGraph, Microsoft Agent Framework, AutoGen, CrewAI, OpenAI Agents SDK) describe per-step or per-superstep snapshots with resume/fork, while a 2025-2026 wave of systems papers (AgentRewind, Crab, DeltaBox, ACRFence, GA-Rollback, WebRollback, GCC, ChronoMem) evaluates checkpoints only on task success, recovery correctness or C/R latency; none measures the exposé's audit tasks (failure localization, evidence tracing, reconstruction, counterfactuals, policy compliance) or Auditable-Agents-style metrics. No paper puts a log-primary and a checkpoint-based design on the same workflow: the only head-to-head framings are grey literature (Augment Code, Zylos) and LogAct's qualitative Table 1, so a small controlled comparison would be genuinely new. A concrete, testable hypothesis falls out of the docs themselves: LangGraph replay re-fires LLM and tool calls (non-deterministic) whereas ActiveGraph, Temporal, Restate and LogAct replay cached outputs, so checkpoint-based systems should score lower on execution-reconstruction fidelity and counterfactual reproducibility unless they also log model outputs, and SymTrace's finding that unguided reruns reproduce only 68% of failures gives a way to measure this. Crab's chat-only vs chat+FS vs full-state ladder offers a ready template for measuring record fidelity of checkpoints, and Microsoft's 'checkpoint store is a trust boundary' (pickle, no hash chain) means checkpoints sit at Evidence Integrity level 0-1, a cheap but unmeasured comparison against hash-chained logs. Finally, the hybrids (DBOS, Flink Agents, LogAct, ACRFence's replay-or-fork) suggest the three-family taxonomy is a spectrum, and the two costs unique to checkpoint counterfactuals, security (semantic rollback attacks) and compute (Zero-Replay), have never been reported in an audit framing.

The auditor literature gives clear, quantified evidence that (a) what the record CONTAINS changes localization accuracy (TraceElephant: full inputs+outputs vs outputs-only lifts step accuracy 16% to 30.3%; Who&When: supplying the final answer helps every protocol), (b) how the same linear log is PRESENTED changes it (ECHO's 4-level hierarchy 6% to 28% step accuracy; all-at-once vs step-by-step vs binary search trade agent- against step-level accuracy; TrajDebug's multi-granularity views), and (c) LLM auditors collapse with record length (Who&When Pro 94% to 50% step accuracy from <3K to >12K tokens; TRAIL r = -0.38; Plan-RewardBench below chance above 32K; positional 'context rot'). However, every one of these studies varies presentation of a chat-style or span-style linear log; none compares the three architecture families of the exposé - event log vs per-step state snapshots vs runtime provenance graph - as the auditor's input on the same controlled runs, so the question 'does the record format itself change failure localization or evidence tracing' is open. The graph-style results (FALAT, AFANet, DRIFT claim-evidence overlays, the zero-replay event knowledge graph) all build the graph post hoc from the log inside the auditor, never contrasting it with a provenance overlay captured at runtime (PROV-AGENT style), and the withdrawal of GraphTracer (arXiv 2510.10581, withdrawn Dec 2025 for a methodological error) is a warning that 'graph beats linear' claims need careful validation. Counterfactual methods (Causal Agent Replay, DoVer, CausalFlow) implicitly require checkpoint/replay capability and DoVer shows log-only hypotheses are often untested, yet nobody quantifies the replay-cost vs reading-cost trade across architectures beyond the zero-replay paper. Human evidence is thin and format-agnostic (AGDebugger n=14 qualitative; AgentGUI 38% faster; the 2.8x XAI result is a single-author preprint), with no time-to-diagnose study across record formats. A feasible term-paper hypothesis therefore is: record one small deterministic workflow with injected faults (trajectory-judge / Who&When Pro style) in three formats - append-only event log, per-step state snapshots with diffs, and a runtime PROV/claim-evidence graph - feed each to the same LLM judge under a matched token budget, and measure step-localization exact match, evidence-tracing PCov/PSnd, reconstruction RF/ACR, plus storage and tokens; the literature predicts a format-by-length interaction (log wins on reconstruction, snapshots on state-corruption faults, provenance graph on evidence tracing and long runs).

The systems literature already contains a crisp, quantitative version of the exposé's research question: checkpointing buys low runtime overhead at the price of high reconstruction cost, logging/lineage does the opposite (Lineage Stash, Elnozahy et al.), the optimal checkpoint spacing follows a square-root law in checkpoint cost and failure rate (Young/Daly), and time-travel latency is proportional to snapshot interval (Tardis). None of the four agent papers in the folder cite or test any of this, so a term paper that imports these formulas as predictions and measures them on one controlled agent workflow (bytes stored, seconds to reconstruct step k, seconds to localize an injected failure, cost of a counterfactual fork at distance d from the end) would be novel enough and cheap to run. A second gap is conceptual: the rollback-recovery literature says log-based replay only works under the piecewise-deterministic assumption, i.e. when every nondeterministic event is logged as a determinant; for LLM agents the determinants are the model outputs and tool results, which are also the bulk of the data, so the classic 'logging is cheap' results (rr, ReVirt) may not transfer and the storage ordering of the three families should be measured rather than assumed. Third, provenance theory (why/how/where, semirings, wat-provenance) predicts that log-only architectures give causal over-approximations for evidence tracing and that counterfactual questions need how-provenance, which means an overlay that stores only support edges should fail counterfactual audits; this is a testable precision difference between families. Finally, in the systems literature hybrids (snapshot + log of external interactions, asynchronous lineage, write-ahead lineage) dominate pure designs, suggesting the paper should either add a checkpoint-plus-log hybrid as a fourth condition or explicitly predict where LangGraph-style checkpointers (which already keep both) sit on the Pareto front.

Every failure-localization benchmark found here (Who&When, TRAIL, TraceElephant, AgentRx, LongRCA, TELBench, CatchBench) fixes one record format per dataset, flat chat logs, OTel spans, or JSON trajectories, and never records the same run under different architectures, so the exposé's core question (does log-primary vs checkpoint vs provenance-overlay change what an auditor can recover?) has no direct evidence; the nearest result is TraceElephant's finding that full traces beat transcripts by up to 76% at step level, and Deepchecks' 3.5x-12.5x gain from span-wise instead of whole-trace judging, both of which show representation matters without saying which architecture supplies it. Ground truth is either costly human labels with low step-level agreement (Krippendorff 0.64 in TraceElephant, 39.5% exact-step agreement in LongRCA) or 'golden' labels manufactured by replaying a successful prefix and injecting one fault (Who&When Pro, AgenTracer, Aegis), and that injection pipeline is itself a replay/fork capability, which means a student can generate step-level ground truth on a small controlled workflow almost for free with ActiveGraph forks or LangGraph checkpoints and then compare architectures on identical injected faults. Log-only LLM judges sit at 11-28% step accuracy on human-labeled data but ~70% on injected data, while intervention-based methods (DoVer, Causal Agent Replay, CausalFlow) argue log-only attribution is untested and often ill-posed and require re-execution from step k, so the literature implicitly splits along the exposé's axis: checkpoint/event-sourced records enable verified counterfactual attribution, provenance overlays enable evidence tracing and decision review (DRIFT, LEDGER), and neither community measures the other's task. No paper reports storage size, replay/re-execution cost, or privacy exposure of the record alongside attribution accuracy, and MP-Bench plus DoVer show that a single gold step may be the wrong target, so scoring counterfactual analysis as 'set of interventions that flip the run' is an open design choice. A concrete, cheap hypothesis: on one fixed workflow with N injected single-point faults, judge step-localization accuracy and DoVer-style flip rate increase from provenance-overlay-only to log-only to log-plus-fork records, and the gain from replay-verified attribution exceeds the gain from richer static records, at a measurable multiple of storage and compute; the checkpoint arm can be represented by LangGraph checkpointing plus DoVer/CAR-style interventions since no checkpoint-family paper exists in this attribution literature.

The privacy/redaction angle reveals that every source handles privacy in a family-specific and unmeasured way: log-primary designs have mature cryptographic options (redactable hash chains via summary hashes or chameleon hashes, per-event disclosure keys in Agent Flight Recorder, hash-only records in the IETF AAT draft, pseudonymization), the OTel/provenance-overlay family relies on collector-side PII scrubbing or dual raw/redacted tables, and the checkpoint family has nothing at all beyond a GitHub issue showing that each LangGraph checkpoint re-serializes the full state (21.8 KB for a 16-turn agent), which means PII is duplicated in every snapshot and no redaction scheme for checkpoints has been proposed. No paper measures what redaction does to the exposé's audit tasks: RedAct is the only quantitative study (91-96.6% retention of verifier fields after removing 70.6% of protected content) but it covers one flat trace format and ignores failure localization, counterfactual replay and policy decidability; Auditable Agents explicitly lists 'which privacy-preserving mechanisms can retain audit-grade fidelity' as unresolved, and its Proposition 1 predicts that hash-only records make whole policy classes undecidable, a prediction nobody has tested against the AAT draft's hash-only mandate. Overhead numbers exist but are incomparable across families (AEGIS 8.3 ms per tool call; AFR 47.6 us and 512 B per event; OTel 3-42% CPU depending on batching; PROV-AGENT reports none), so a single controlled workflow reporting bytes-per-step and per-step latency for all three architectures would itself be a contribution. A feasible, testable hypothesis for a one-semester paper is therefore: under three recording levels (raw, field-level redaction, hash-only), evidence tracing and semantic policy checking degrade sharply while execution reconstruction and integrity verification are preserved, and the degradation curve differs by architecture, with checkpoint-based systems losing counterfactual fidelity first (because redacted state cannot be replayed) and provenance overlays losing evidence tracing first (because grouping breaks source-to-claim paths). The From-Agent-Traces-to-Trust survey's note that checkpoint rollback can produce semantically unsafe re-executions adds a second cheap sub-hypothesis about replaying redacted checkpoints.
